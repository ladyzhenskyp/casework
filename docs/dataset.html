Support operations dataset

The data behind version 1: a fictional software company's support team, six months of tickets, and three systems that don't fully agree. Everything here is synthetic. The data has been generated and every case's answer has been checked against it.

The company

Northwind Desk (invented) sells scheduling software to small and mid-size businesses on three plans: basic, pro and enterprise. Its support team of 24 agents answers customers by email, chat, web form and phone. The data covers April through September 2026.

The setup that causes trouble

Sales and billing live in the CRM. Support works in the helpdesk. An integration copies each customer's plan from the CRM to the helpdesk every night, and the helpdesk uses that copy to decide which response-time promise (SLA) applies. When the sync fails, the two systems drift apart.

Approximate size

300 customers, 323 helpdesk organizations, 24 agents, 5,852 tickets, 23,789 ticket events, 2,060 survey responses and 3,136 sync attempts. All eight tables together are 428 KB, small enough to load in a browser in about a second and big enough that problems can't be spotted by eye.

How the tables connect
  • helpdesk_orgs.crm_customer_id to customers.customer_id (not always filled in, sometimes twice)
  • tickets.org_id to helpdesk_orgs.org_id, and tickets.assignee_id to agents.agent_id
  • ticket_events.ticket_id to tickets.ticket_id
  • csat_responses.ticket_id to tickets.ticket_id (some point at tickets that don't exist)
  • integration_sync_log.customer_id to customers.customer_id
  • sla_policies joins on plan tier and priority to give the response-time target

Planted problems

The mess learners have to find. Each one is a real thing that goes wrong on support teams, and each shows up in at least one case.

Problem 1

Auto-replies counted as responses

The "we got your message" email fires in seconds. A naive first-response time counts it, so the team looks fast and SLAs look met.

ticket_events.event_type = 'auto_reply'
Problem 2

Reopened tickets counted as solved

About 7% of solved tickets get reopened, and integration tickets about 17%. Reports that only check the final status miss that the first fix didn't work.

ticket_events.event_type = 'reopened'
Problem 3

Wrong plan in the helpdesk

About 20 customers upgraded in the CRM, but the helpdesk still has their old plan, so their tickets run on a slower SLA clock.

customers.plan_tier vs helpdesk_orgs.sla_tier
Problem 4

Failed syncs behind the drift

The integration's credentials expired for 6 days in July (401 errors), and rate limiting (429) dropped some retries. That's why the plans drifted.

integration_sync_log.http_status
Problem 5

Phone tickets in the wrong time zone

Phone tickets were logged in New York time while everything else is UTC, so every phone ticket looks 4 hours slower than it really was and the dashboard flags false SLA breaches.

tickets.channel = 'phone'
Problem 6

Messy category labels

Agents type categories by hand, so one topic appears five ways, plus blanks. Volume reports undercount the real top issues.

tickets.category
Problem 7

Duplicate tickets

Customers email, then open a chat about the same problem minutes later. Some got merged, many didn't, which inflates volume.

same requester and topic within 30 minutes
Problem 8

Tickets owned by people who left

Three agents left mid-period. 30 of their tickets were never reassigned and are quietly aging.

agents.deactivated_at is not null
Problem 9

Untrustworthy survey data

Some survey responses point at tickets that don't exist, and some tickets were rated twice. Averages that ignore this are off.

csat_responses.ticket_id
Problem 10

Customers missing from one system

14 trial accounts have no CRM link, 3 links are mistyped, and 6 customers have two helpdesk orgs, which splits their history.

helpdesk_orgs.crm_customer_id

Answer checking

How the app decides a learner's result matches the expected one. These are the rules I recommended earlier, now applied to this data.

Row order doesn't count

Unless the case asks for a sorted list (like case 01's oldest-first queue), any order passes.

Column names don't count

Only the values, column by column, in order. Naming a column n instead of tickets still passes.

Numbers match to the answer key's precision

If the key says 153.3, a result of 153.27 passes too. Cases say how to round when it matters.

Duplicate rows do count

Returning a ticket twice is a real bug, so it fails, and the tutor says which rows were repeated.

Time units are stated in the case

Durations are always asked for in minutes, so nobody fails on hours versus minutes.

Mismatch explanations come from the comparison

The app works out which rows are missing, extra or different. The AI tutor only puts that into plain words.
